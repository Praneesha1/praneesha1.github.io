Overview

I built a four cylinder inline engine in Fusion 360. I modeled each part myself. The main parts are the engine block, the crankshaft, four pistons, four connecting rods, four rod caps, four piston pins, and eight pin clips. I put all the parts together in one assembly. I used joints so each part sits in the right place.

How it moves

The crankshaft has a revolute joint. The pistons slide inside the cylinders. When the crankshaft turns, the pistons move up and down. Pistons 1 and 4 move together. Pistons 2 and 3 move together, half a turn apart. This is the normal layout for an inline four.

The motion in the viewer above is not a recording. It is computed from the real geometry of the model. The crank radius, the rod length, and the cylinder spacing all come from the exported parts. The 2D drawing on the right runs from the same crank angle.

Drawings and views

I made front, top, left, right, and section views. I made an exploded view to show how the parts fit together. I made a drawing sheet with all the views on one page. The drawing sheet is at 1:50 scale. The material listed is steel.

What I learned

  • Big assemblies need every part fully constrained. One loose joint breaks the whole motion.
  • Section views show problems that normal views hide.
  • Naming parts early saves a lot of time later.
Tip: open the Parts panel in the viewer and press "only" next to the crankshaft. Then press Play.
